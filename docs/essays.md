# Part 2: Short Essays

## 1. What's something we wouldn't know about you just by looking at your resume?

While my resume highlights the algorithms I built for China Fun Restaurants, it doesn't capture the reality of the years I spent managing the floor. You won't see the nights spent calming angry customers during a dinner rush, mediating disputes between kitchen staff, or scrubbing fryers when we were short-staffed.

This experience gave me a "service-first" engineering philosophy. When I built the inventory forecasting model, I wasn't just optimizing a Python script; I was trying to save the prep cooks from staying late to throw away unused food. I learned that the best software doesn't just run efficiently; it respects the labor of the people using it. This background has made me an engineer who prioritizes user empathy and operational reality just as much as Big O notation. I build tools to solve human problems, not just technical ones.

## 2. What are you looking for in an internship?

I have frequently worn the hat of "Lead Engineer" out of necessity, architecting full-stack solutions for my startup and internships. However, I am seeking an internship to experience the rigor of an established engineering culture. When you build alone, you can move fast, but you often miss the blind spots that only a senior engineer's code review can reveal.

I am looking for an environment where "it works" is not the bar for success—where maintainability, scalability, and clean code principles are enforced. I want to have my design patterns challenged by mentors who have seen systems fail at a scale I haven't touched yet. My goal is to transition from being a capable "builder" to a disciplined "engineer," learning the industry standards that turn a working prototype into reliable, long-term software infrastructure.
